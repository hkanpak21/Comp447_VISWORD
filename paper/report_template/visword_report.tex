\begin{abstract}
We study whether visual encoders that read documents as images can rival
or surpass text encoders that read the same content as tokens, using a
Wikipedia-screenshot retrieval benchmark
(\texttt{Tevatron/wiki-ss-corpus}) as the testbed. Our setup pairs a
leak-free crop$\to$page protocol (Protocol~A) with a fair
ViT-B-scale comparison across encoders trained with five distinct
objectives: supervised classification (ImageNet ViT), self-supervised
view-contrastive (DINOv2), image--text contrastive (CLIP, softmax
InfoNCE), image--text contrastive (SigLIP, sigmoid pairwise), and
image-only predictive (I-JEPA). We frame three questions. \textbf{(Q1)}
When and why does a visual encoder outperform a strong BERT-style text
encoder on the same documents? \textbf{(Q2)} Can a JEPA-style
predictive image encoder, which never sees text during pretraining,
attain CLIP-level multimodal alignment through a small post-hoc text
adapter? \textbf{(Q3)} How does the Platonic Representation Hypothesis
\cite{huh2024platonic} explain the cross-encoder ordering, and does
LEACE-style causal erasure \cite{belrose2023leace} of layout-region or
title-region directions disambiguate ``encoded'' from ``causally used''
features? Headline zero-shot finding ($P{=}2000$ pages, single seed): CLIP
zero-shot achieves Protocol-A R@10$\,=\,0.779$, an enormous margin
over DINOv2 (0.058), I-JEPA (0.015 — at chance), and ImageNet-supervised
ViT (0.031). The gap rules out ``architecture alone explains the
result'' (random ViT scores 0.029) and forces the explanatory burden
onto pretraining objective. The Q3 question is left open by this
evidence in an interesting way: I-JEPA's image-only predictive
features are uncompetitive out-of-the-box on visual document
retrieval, so the relevant test is whether a small post-hoc text
adapter \cite{jose2024dinov2text} can rescue I-JEPA to CLIP-level
performance --- and crucially, do so without the OCR-style shortcut
behaviour CLIP is known to fall into
\cite{lin2024parrot, materzynska2022spelling}. Title-blanked Phase-2
and LEACE causal erasure (\S\ref{sec:exp_titleblanked},
\S\ref{sec:exp_leace}) disambiguate the H-OCR / H-Platonic /
H-Form hypotheses for CLIP's edge.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A document is a hybrid object: a sequence of glyphs that together form
text, embedded in a visual layout that itself carries meaning (titles,
infoboxes, captions, figures, tables). Retrieval over documents has
two natural extremes. On one end, text-only encoders such as
BERT~\cite{devlin2019bert} read OCR'd or extracted text and ignore
layout. On the other end, image-only encoders such as DINOv2
\cite{oquab2024dinov2} or CLIP's image branch~\cite{radford2021clip}
treat the document as a screenshot and ignore the explicit token
stream. Pixel-based language models~\cite{rust2023pixel,
tschannen2023clippo, lee2023pix2struct} sit in between, training on
rendered text but evaluated on standard NLP benchmarks. ColPali
\cite{faysse2025colpali} recently showed that direct image-only
retrieval over screenshots, with late interaction, can be competitive
with strong OCR+text pipelines on document QA.

This paper takes the visual-document-retrieval task as a controlled
setting in which to ask a specific cluster of questions about
\emph{what visual encoders learn that text encoders miss, and why}.
We organise the contribution around three positioning questions.

\paragraph{Q1 (visual vs.\ text encoders).} Why might a vision encoder
that reads the document as a screenshot retrieve documents
\emph{better} than a text encoder that reads the underlying text? The
naive expectation is the opposite: BERT can read every glyph; a
14$\times$14-pixel patch encoder operating on a 224$\times$224 crop
cannot resolve body text. Yet several mechanisms make the visual side
competitive: (i)~visual encoders capture \emph{layout structure}
(presence of an infobox, position of a header, list bullets, figure
placement) that text-only encoders discard during plaintext
extraction; (ii)~visual encoders are immune to OCR errors,
out-of-vocabulary tokens, multilingual scripts, formulas, and
non-Latin glyphs; (iii)~when the query itself is a visual crop
(scrolling through a long page, snapshot of a section), the visual
encoder is the natural matcher. Our experimental task isolates these
mechanisms because the query is a $224\times224$ crop with no
side-channel text and the gallery is the source-page representation.
We measure layout-class linear probe accuracy as a direct test of
mechanism~(i).

\paragraph{Q2 (predictive vs.\ contrastive multimodal).}
Image--text contrastive pretraining (CLIP, SigLIP) requires
billion-scale (image, caption) corpora and produces representations
heavily entangled with the OCR-like behaviour of glyph
recognition~\cite{lin2024parrot, materzynska2022spelling}. JEPA-family
encoders (I-JEPA~\cite{assran2023ijepa},
V-JEPA~\cite{bardes2024vjepa}) achieve self-supervision by predicting
masked-block representations in latent space, requiring no text and
no augmentation invariance. We ask whether a JEPA-pretrained image
encoder can attain CLIP-level cross-modal retrieval performance
through a small post-hoc text adapter trained only on
(\texttt{page-screenshot}, \texttt{page-title}) pairs from
Wikipedia~\cite{jose2024dinov2text}. If yes, this provides a
text-supervision-free path to multimodal alignment.

\paragraph{Q3 (Platonic alignment as the explanatory frame).} The
Platonic Representation Hypothesis~\cite{huh2024platonic} predicts
that encoders trained on diverse objectives converge toward a shared
representation, and that alignment between an image encoder and a
text encoder should \emph{predict} downstream cross-modal performance.
We measure debiased CKA~\cite{murphy2024align}, mutual-kNN, and
Procrustes distance for every \emph{(image encoder, text encoder)}
pair on a fixed eval slice and ask whether the alignment ordering
matches the retrieval ordering. We further use
LEACE~\cite{belrose2023leace} to causally erase the title-region
direction and the layout-class direction from each encoder's
descriptors and re-measure both retrieval and Platonic alignment, so
encoded features are distinguished from causally used ones.

\paragraph{Contributions.} (1)~A leak-free crop$\to$page retrieval
protocol with leave-one-crop-out gallery aggregation that, when
applied to four previously trained checkpoints, drops headline
R@10 from \TODO{0.92$^*$} to \TODO{0.YY} and exposes the
template-fingerprint inflation of the original metric. (2)~A
ViT-B-scale fair-comparison grid across five pretraining objectives
plus a random-init sanity floor, evaluated zero-shot and after
last-4-block fine-tuning, with the resulting cross-encoder
performance ordering. (3)~A Platonic-alignment grid across five
image and four text encoders on the same eval slice, paired with a
LEACE causal-erasure analysis showing which directions in each
encoder's representation are causally used for retrieval.
(4)~A title-blanked Phase-2 protocol that quantifies
``CLIP-as-OCR'' behaviour: the drop in R@1 when the top 15\,\% of
every anchor and pool image is whited out. (5)~A negative finding
on perceptual-hash visual deduplication of Wikipedia screenshots
that motivates content-based dedup as future work.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Pixel-based language models and visual document AI}

PIXEL~\cite{rust2023pixel} re-renders text into images and reaches
BERT-level GLUE on English while exceeding it on non-Latin scripts.
CLIPPO~\cite{tschannen2023clippo} trains a single CLIP-style ViT on
image-and-rendered-alt-text, demonstrating that a contrastive
objective alone suffices to learn meaningful pixel-text alignment.
Pix2Struct~\cite{lee2023pix2struct} parses masked webpage screenshots
into simplified HTML; PIXAR~\cite{tai2024pixar} extends this to
autoregressive generation. Lotz et al.~\cite{lotz2023rendering}
systematically studied rendering and discovered an anisotropic
patch-embedding space driven by patch-frequency bias, the visual
analogue of subword frequency effects. PIXEL-M4~\cite{kesen2025pixelm4}
extended this multilingually.

In document AI proper, ColPali~\cite{faysse2025colpali} produces
ColBERT-style multi-vector embeddings of full pages and outperforms
strong text+OCR pipelines on the ViDoRe benchmark, suggesting
aggregation may be the wrong design choice for document retrieval.
DocOwl-1.5~\cite{hu2024docowl} introduces an H-Reducer that merges
adjacent patches, addressing the same aggregation question SALAD
\cite{izquierdo2024salad} answers in a place-recognition
context. Donut~\cite{kim2022donut}, Nougat~\cite{blecher2023nougat},
and LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3} provide the OCR-free
document-understanding baselines.

\subsection{Self-supervised visual representation and the
DINOv2$\to$text transfer}

DINOv2~\cite{oquab2024dinov2} produces high-quality patch features
through a view-contrastive distillation objective with no text supervision.
Jose et al.~\cite{jose2024dinov2text} showed that a small text
encoder aligned post-hoc to a \emph{frozen} DINOv2 already reaches
CLIP-level zero-shot classification: strong evidence that
self-supervised image features already contain a linguistically
decodable structure. I-JEPA~\cite{assran2023ijepa} replaces the
view-invariance objective with a latent-prediction one (predict
masked target-block representations from context-block
representations) and avoids both contrastive and reconstruction
losses; V-JEPA~\cite{bardes2024vjepa} extends to video. Whether
JEPA's predictive objective induces text-alignable features as well
as DINOv2's contrastive one does is, to our knowledge, untested.
This paper's Q2 is one such test on document screenshots.

\subsection{Image--text contrastive pretraining and OCR shortcuts}

CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip} learns a shared image--text embedding via
contrastive learning over web image--caption pairs; SigLIP
\cite{zhai2023siglip} replaces CLIP's softmax InfoNCE with a sigmoid
pairwise loss, enabling scaling and yielding small but consistent
improvements on zero-shot benchmarks. Both have been shown to ``read''
text rendered in images: Goh et al.~\cite{goh2021typographic}
introduced typographic attacks, Materzy{\'n}ska
et al.~\cite{materzynska2022spelling} demonstrated that spelling and
meaning are partially separable subspaces in CLIP, and Lin et
al.~\cite{lin2024parrot} estimated that $\sim$50\,\% of LAION-2B
captions verbatim repeat text-in-image, explaining CLIP's strong
glyph-recognition behaviour. Yuksekgonul et
al.~\cite{yuksekgonul2023bagofwords} showed CLIP/BLIP/FLAVA behave
like bags-of-words on the ARO benchmark. We exploit these findings to
design a title-blanked Phase-2 test (\S\ref{sec:exp_titleblanked})
that quantifies how much of CLIP's retrieval signal on Wikipedia
screenshots comes from OCR'ing the page title.

\subsection{Platonic representation hypothesis and probing methodology}

The Platonic Representation Hypothesis~\cite{huh2024platonic} predicts
that as encoders are trained on more data and more diverse objectives,
they converge to a shared representation, and that alignment between
two encoders' representations on the same content should correlate
with downstream cross-modal performance. Maniparambil et
al.~\cite{maniparambil2024crossencoder} measured this alignment for
natural-image vision encoders and corresponding text encoders;
Moayeri et al.~\cite{moayeri2023text2concept} provided a single linear
map (Text2Concept) that operationalises alignment for downstream use.
Murphy et al.~\cite{murphy2024align} pointed out that vanilla CKA is
biased and proposed a debiased estimator we adopt here. We use these
metrics, plus mutual-kNN and Procrustes distance, on the
\emph{document-screenshot} domain rather than natural images, treating
the visual content--text content pairing as an unusually clean test
case (the visual content \emph{is} the text).

For probing, we follow the now-canonical methodology: classical
linear probes~\cite{alain2017probing, conneau2018probing} report
information \emph{encoded}; control-task selectivity
\cite{hewitt2019designing} and MDL probes~\cite{voita2020mdl} avoid
the probe-capacity confound; LEACE~\cite{belrose2023leace} provides
a closed-form, optimal linear erasure that distinguishes
\emph{causally used} features from merely encoded ones, building on
the INLP~\cite{ravfogel2020inlp},
Amnesic~\cite{elazar2021amnesic}, and RLACE~\cite{ravfogel2022rlace}
line of causal-probing work. We do not run sparse-autoencoder
analyses~\cite{cunningham2024saes, lieberum2024gemmascope,
stevens2025saev} in this paper but flag them as the natural next
step (Section~\ref{sec:disc}).

\section{Approach}
\label{sec:approach}

\subsection{Task and dataset}
\label{sec:task}

We use \texttt{Tevatron/wiki-ss-corpus}: $\sim$1.2\,M Wikipedia page
screenshots paired with the page \texttt{title} and rendered body
\texttt{text}. We hold a local cache of $\sim$376\,k pages
(\texttt{compress\_level=1} PNG, sha256-fingerprinted manifest). For
the anchor-pool transfer evaluation we use
\texttt{hkanpak21/Wikipedia\_SS\_withanchors}, a 571-anchor validation
set in which each anchor crop is paired with positives (other crops
of the same page) and hard negatives (crops of unrelated pages); the
effective subset on disk is $\sim$118 anchors due to upstream image
availability constraints.

A page is rendered at a typical width of $\sim$1024 pixels and
variable height (3\,000--6\,000 pixels for typical articles). Crops
are produced by a non-overlapping cropper at $490\times490$ pixels
and resized to $224\times224$ for ViT-B/14 or B/16 inputs. At this
resolution, individual letter strokes are 1--2 pixels wide and body
text is sub-pixel (illegible to the model); only headings and the
overall layout structure remain visually recoverable. Any claim of
``the model is reading'' should be read as ``the model is reading
heading-level text and using layout fingerprints,'' not body content.

\subsection{Protocol A: leak-free crop$\to$page retrieval}
\label{sec:protocol_a}

Earlier work on this corpus reported a Phase-1 R@10 of 0.92$^*$ for a
DINOv2+SALAD recipe, but used a crop-vs-crop nearest-neighbour
formulation in which $\sim$10--20 same-page positives existed in a
pool of $\sim$20\,k crops; ``find any same-page neighbour in top
10'' is much easier than ``find the source page of a crop.'' We
replace this with \emph{Protocol~A}:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item For each eval page $p$ with crops $\{c_p^{(1)}, \dots, c_p^{(n_p)}\}$,
        compute the gallery vector
        $g_p = \operatorname{normalise}\!\bigl(\sum_i \phi(c_p^{(i)})\bigr)$
        where $\phi$ is the encoder's L2-normed crop embedding.
  \item For a query crop $c$ on page $q$, recompute that page's
        gallery as $g_q^{\setminus c}
        = \operatorname{normalise}\!\bigl(\sum_i \phi(c_q^{(i)}) - \phi(c)\bigr)$
        (leave-one-crop-out aggregation).
  \item Cosine-rank the $P$ gallery vectors against $\phi(c)$;
        report R@$k$ as the fraction of queries whose true source
        page lands in the top $k$.
  \item Restrict queries to crops with text density $\geq 5\,\%$
        (the training distribution) so blank/margin crops, which
        the model never saw labelled, are not queries.
\end{enumerate}

With $P=2000$ pages, random R@1 is $1/2000 = 5\times 10^{-4}$ — a
real retrieval task. Re-evaluating four previously trained
checkpoints under Protocol~A yields the corrected numbers in
Section~\ref{sec:exp_retrieval}.

\subsection{Encoder ladder (ViT-B fair comparison)}
\label{sec:encoders}

We compare five image encoders, all at ViT-B scale:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{ImageNet-21k+1k ViT-B/16} (\texttt{timm}'s
        \texttt{vit\_base\_patch16\_224.augreg\_in21k\_ft\_in1k}):
        supervised classification baseline.
  \item \textbf{DINOv2 ViT-B/14}~\cite{oquab2024dinov2}:
        self-supervised view-contrastive distillation.
  \item \textbf{CLIP ViT-B/16}~\cite{radford2021clip}: image--text
        contrastive with softmax InfoNCE on $\sim$400\,M LAION-style
        pairs.
  \item \textbf{SigLIP ViT-B/16}~\cite{zhai2023siglip}: image--text
        contrastive with sigmoid pairwise loss; isolates loss form
        from CLIP.
  \item \textbf{I-JEPA ViT-H/14}~\cite{assran2023ijepa}: image-only
        latent-prediction objective with no text supervision. The
        publicly released I-JEPA models are H-scale; we document
        this asymmetry and exclude I-JEPA from the
        matched-architecture sub-comparisons.
  \item \textbf{Random-init ViT-B/16} (deterministic seed 1234,
        frozen): sanity floor.
\end{itemize}

For text encoders we use BERT-base~\cite{devlin2019bert},
MiniLM~\cite{wang2020minilm}, CLIP-text, and SigLIP-text. All are
held frozen for zero-shot evaluation; for fine-tuning we train the
last four blocks of the image backbone plus the head, with a
discriminative learning rate ($\eta_{\text{bb}}=10^{-5}$ for
image-only, $10^{-7}$ for image--text encoders to avoid the
catastrophic collapse we observe with $10^{-5}$ on CLIP).

\subsection{Heads and aggregation}
\label{sec:heads}

We pair each encoder with two heads: (i)~an MLP CLS head
(768$\to$512$\to$256, L2-normed), and (ii)~the official SALAD
optimal-transport aggregator~\cite{izquierdo2024salad}, an 8\,448-d
descriptor formed from a token-CLS branch and a 64-cluster VLAD
branch with a Sinkhorn assignment and a learnable ``dustbin'' for
uninformative patches. SALAD's per-cluster aggregation provides a
useful interpretability hook (cluster-level attention, dustbin
mass over training); details are deferred to
\S\ref{sec:exp_interpret}.

\subsection{Platonic alignment measurement}
\label{sec:platonic_method}

For each (image encoder $E_v$, text encoder $E_t$) pair, we encode
the same fixed slice of $N=1000$ Wikipedia pages: image features
$\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times d_v}$ from page-screenshot
encoding, text features
$\mathbf{Y} \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times d_t}$ from page-title encoding.
We report:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Debiased CKA}~\cite{kornblith2019cka,
        murphy2024align}: kernel-based representational
        similarity, debiased to avoid the small-$N$ bias that
        inflates classical CKA.
  \item \textbf{Procrustes distance}: orthogonal-Procrustes optimal
        $\|\mathbf{X}R - \mathbf{Y}\|_F$, normalised to $[0, 1]$.
  \item \textbf{Mutual $k$-NN overlap}: fraction of points sharing
        at least one of their top-$k$ neighbours under the two
        embeddings, at $k=5$ and $k=10$.
\end{itemize}

The Platonic Hypothesis predicts a positive correlation between
alignment scores and downstream Protocol-A R@10 across encoders;
\S\ref{sec:exp_platonic} tests this directly.

\subsection{LEACE causal erasure}
\label{sec:leace_method}

We use LEACE~\cite{belrose2023leace} for closed-form linear
erasure of two protected directions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Title-region direction}: estimated as the mean
        difference between the original-image embedding and the
        title-blanked embedding (Section~\ref{sec:exp_titleblanked}),
        a one-dimensional projection along the gradient of
        ``what changes when we erase the title.''
  \item \textbf{Layout-type direction}: the column-space of a
        five-way one-hot indicator (header / infobox / body /
        list / figure), labelled by the spatial region from
        which each crop was sampled.
\end{enumerate}

For each erased direction we report (i)~Protocol-A R@10
post-erasure and (ii)~Platonic alignment with each text encoder
post-erasure. A direction is \emph{causally used} for retrieval
when erasing it drops R@10 substantially; merely \emph{encoded}
when erasing leaves R@10 roughly unchanged.

\subsection{JEPA + post-hoc text adapter (Q2)}
\label{sec:adapter_method}

To test whether I-JEPA's image-only features are post-hoc
text-alignable in the manner of DINOv2~\cite{jose2024dinov2text}, we
freeze the I-JEPA encoder and train a small linear adapter
$A \in \mathbb{R}^{d_v \times 768}$ mapping I-JEPA image features
into the embedding space of a frozen text encoder $E_t$
(BERT-base or CLIP-text). The training objective is contrastive on
(\texttt{page-screenshot}, \texttt{page-title}) pairs from
wiki-ss with InfoNCE temperature $\tau=0.07$. We ask whether the
adapted I-JEPA embedding $A E_v(c)$ matches the retrieval
performance of zero-shot CLIP image features. If yes, this provides
a text-supervision-free path to multimodal alignment that
side-steps the OCR shortcut documented for CLIP.

\subsection{Visual vs.\ BERT: when do screenshots beat text?}
\label{sec:exp_visual_vs_bert}

The most important comparison for Q1 is not visual-vs-visual but
\emph{visual-vs-text}. For a fair side-by-side, we encode the same
pages two ways: (a)~as screenshots through each visual encoder, and
(b)~as page titles through each text encoder. We then evaluate two
retrieval modes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Mode I (image$\to$image).} Protocol-A on visual
        encoders. Text encoders cannot participate.
  \item \textbf{Mode II (image$\to$text).} Cross-modal: query is a
        crop image, gallery is the page-title text encoded by
        BERT/MiniLM/CLIP-text/SigLIP-text. The image encoder must
        be aligned with the text space (zero-shot CLIP and SigLIP
        do this natively; DINOv2 and I-JEPA require an adapter,
        \S\ref{sec:adapter_method}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{When does visual win?} We hypothesise that visual
encoders win in three regimes:
(i)~when text extraction is unreliable (low-quality scans,
multilingual content, formula-heavy pages);
(ii)~when the query is itself a visual crop with no associated
text;
(iii)~when layout structure carries information the text-only
encoder discards.
We probe (iii) directly with a layout-class linear probe on the
cached descriptors of each encoder: 5-way classification across
\{header, infobox, body, list, figure\} regions. \TODO{Probe
accuracies will be reported once descriptor cache populates.} The
prediction is that visual encoders score substantially higher
than text encoders on layout-class probing, while text encoders
dominate topic-class probing.

\paragraph{When does text win.} For our task, BERT-on-titles
trivially scores R@1$=1.0$ on Phase-2 because every Wikipedia
page has a unique title string that is a verbatim substring of
itself. This degenerate case illustrates a real point: when the
text query and the document text are tokenwise close, no
visual encoder can compete. The interesting regime is the one
visual encoders open up — query and content in different
modalities or with text only partially recoverable.

\subsection{Causal probing with LEACE}

protected directions in turn: (i)~the title-region direction
estimated from the title-blanked variant (one-dimensional);

and re-measure Platonic alignment with the four text encoders.

both R@10 and alignment substantially; merely \emph{encoded}
when only the linear probe for that direction loses

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:disc}

\subsection{What our results would and would not show}

If Tables~\ref{tab:retrieval_zeroshot}--\ref{tab:platonic_grid} hold
the predicted ordering, the central claim is:
\emph{multimodal contrastive pretraining (CLIP, SigLIP) gives a
quantifiable retrieval advantage on visual documents that is
explained by Platonic alignment with text encoders, and most of
that advantage can be recovered by a JEPA-pretrained image-only
encoder plus a small post-hoc text adapter.} The remaining
advantage (whatever fraction of CLIP's lead does not transfer to
adapted I-JEPA) we conjecture is exactly the OCR-shortcut margin
that title-blanked Phase-2 would isolate. Confirming both numerical
predictions would tell a coherent story; falsifying any single
piece would force a clear revision.

\subsection{Limitations}

(1)~All evaluations are single-seed; multi-seed CIs are deferred
(Section~\ref{sec:future}). Cross-encoder differences within
$\sim 0.02$ R@10 should be read as ``within typical seed noise.''
(2)~Eval pool $P=2000$ is small relative to industrial document
corpora; recall numbers will not directly compare to
ColPali-on-ViDoRe~\cite{faysse2025colpali} or document-AI
benchmarks at the $10^5$--$10^6$ scale.
(3)~Crop resolution prevents body-text recovery; ``reading'' in
this paper refers to heading-level text and layout structure.
(4)~Phase-2 effective sample size is $\sim 118$ anchors, not 571,
because the upstream HF anchors repository ships only the
alphabetically-first $\sim 10\,$k anchor images; we report Wilson
intervals on R@1 to surface this.
(5)~I-JEPA's only publicly released weights are ViT-H/14, breaking
the ViT-B fair-comparison policy; we therefore include I-JEPA in
the Platonic alignment grid but exclude it from architecturally
matched retrieval comparisons.
(6)~Perceptual-hash dedup of wiki-ss screenshots produces clusters
dominated by Wikipedia template structure rather than content
near-duplicates. Content-based (text-similarity) dedup is the
right approach and is deferred.

\subsection{Future work}
\label{sec:future}

The §7 program of the project plan continues from this paper:
multi-seed reruns of the headline grid (CIs);
ColPali-style late-interaction baseline (skip aggregation);
sparse-autoencoder analysis of each encoder following
Stevens et al.~\cite{stevens2025saev} for direct feature inventory;
cross-corpus generalisation evaluation (ViDoRe, DocVQA) to
strengthen the ``not just wiki-template-fitting'' claim;
larger-scale tier (CLIP-L, SigLIP-L, I-JEPA-H all frozen) on the
Platonic grid to test the Huh et al.~\cite{huh2024platonic}
``scale $\to$ alignment'' prediction; and a JEPA + cross-modal
adapter that uses the SALAD aggregator's per-cluster representation
to localise alignment to image regions, not just the global
descriptor.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We treated visual document retrieval on Wikipedia screenshots as a
controlled testbed for three positioning questions about visual
encoders: when does visual reading beat tokenwise reading, can
JEPA-style predictive pretraining attain CLIP-level multimodal
alignment without text supervision, and does the Platonic
Representation Hypothesis explain the cross-encoder ordering. We
introduced a leak-free crop$\to$page protocol and a fair
ViT-B-scale encoder grid to make these questions answerable, and a
LEACE-based causal-probing pipeline to disambiguate encoded from
causally-used features. \TODO{Headline numerical claims will be
